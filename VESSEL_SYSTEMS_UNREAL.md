# Ship engineering port

`USPVesselSystemsComponent` is the native Unreal simulation for the *engineering portion* of the original `source/systems.js`, cross-checked with Unity's `VesselSystems.cs`. It is a Blueprint-spawnable actor component. The six original subsystems, twelve power segments, four named presets, NAV/SCM modes, reactor and cooler dependencies, pressure, hull-linked component damage, isolated field repair, finite spares, thermal wear and dock/landing service gate are implemented in C++.

The component does **not** tick independently. The ship controller should create/attach it, call `ResetToDefaults` when a new vessel is created, then call `StepSystems(DeltaSeconds, Inputs)` on its authoritative flight update. Inputs use percentages for hull/shields and `0..1` for thrust, ship heat, weapon heat and cruise spool. Feed `Output.EngineFactor` into thrust, `Output.CoolingFactor` into combat cooling, `Output.ShieldFactor` into shield recharge, `Output.WeaponFactor` into ship weapons, and apply `Output.ShipHeat01` and `Output.ShieldPercent` back to flight/combat state. Honor `bDisarmWeapons`, `bCancelCruise` and `bCancelJump`. Also call `CanFireShipWeapons` before firing. NAV discharges shields at 16 percentage points per second and inhibits ship weapons. A deliberate `SetMode(SCM)` requests cruise/jump cancellation on the next step; a jump already in transit rejects mode changes.

Cockpit engineering MFDs can call `GetTelemetry`, `GetComponentStatus`, `GetFactor`, `GetAllocation`, `ApplyPreset`, `AdjustAllocation`, `SetMode`, `ToggleComponent` and `StartRepair`. A damaged component must be isolated before its health can be restored; field repair consumes exactly one spare for one health point at three points per second. Only a landed or docked ship with the pilot aboard may call `TryService` successfully. The component only resets its own engineering state; the flight/combat controller remains responsible for hull repair, fuel, ammo, credits and station services. `CaptureState` and `RestoreState` provide a validated compact SaveGame payload but are not yet wired to a project save slot.

The original JavaScript's generic `clamp()` default range is `0..1`, including one call for cabin pressure. Unity treats pressure as `0..100` and its MFD shows a percentage. The Unreal component uses the intended percentage range, so pressure drops from 100 gradually after life support fails instead of jumping to 1 after one frame. Long frames are capped to 0.1 seconds per source update. The controller should run regular steps rather than assuming one large call catches up missed time.

`SpacePatriot.VesselSystems.SourceParity` tests the engineering preset budget, deterministic allocation transfer, reactor/power dependencies, NAV shields and weapon gating, finite field repair, service gate, hull-linked damage, pressure warning, cooler/thermal behavior, and snapshot validation. This is a tested simulation layer, not a working in-game engineering station yet. The original `systems.js` also handles bridge walking, interior positions and collision, doors, stations, airlocks, and cockpit camera/recoil; those are outside this component and still require a playable Blueprint integration. Physical repair actions, cockpit mesh/screens/knobs, network authority, ship damage effects and menus also need integration and visual verification in the playable map.

Verification on UE 5.8: `Build.bat SpacePatriotUnrealEditor Win64 Development` succeeded, and headless `SpacePatriot.VesselSystems.SourceParity` ran 1/1 with `Result={Success}`. The native component has not yet been attached to the Kestrel Blueprint or a physical cockpit MFD.
